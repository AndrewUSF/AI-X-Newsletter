Landmark Rulings in Tort Litigation Against Giant Tech Companies

Category: Litigation / Platform Liability

Image Source: ChatGPT

In K.G.M. v. Meta et al., a plaintiff sued Meta and Google in Los Angeles, alleging the social media platforms were negligently designed with features (such as infinite scroll and algorithmic recommendations) that caused addictive use and mental health harms like anxiety, depression, and body-image issues. A jury in March 2026 found both companies negligent in their product design, awarding approximately $3 million in compensatory damages, with Meta allocated the larger share of liability. Punitive damages are to be decided at a later stage.

A separate case in State of New Mexico v. Meta Platforms, Inc. was brought by the state’s Attorney General. The complaint asserted that Meta misled consumers about the safety of Facebook, Instagram, and other services and enabled child sexual exploitation and unsafe interactions on its platforms. A jury in March 2026 found Meta liable on claims under state consumer protection law, concluding that the company’s conduct harmed children and violated statutory duties. The jury awarded $375 million in civil penalties.

These verdicts arise amid a broader wave of lawsuits asserting that Meta’s platforms cause harm through design and business practices. Plaintiffs and state prosecutors have focused on causes of action related to products liability, negligence and consumer laws, bypassing immunities granted by law to providers of computer services from liability related to user generated content.

Implications/bottom line: The two rulings are viewed as bellwether cases, that will affect numerous pending and future similar legal actions against the tech giants.
