New AI Literacy Framework by the Department of Labor

The Department of Labor recently released its Artificial Intelligence Literacy Framework, encouraging education systems and workplaces to integrate AI literacy in a practical and measurable way.

The framework emphasizes five foundational skills, including understanding AI principles and using AI responsibly, alongside seven delivery strategies such as experiential learning, learning in context, and design for agility.

Implication: May influence curriculum design and federal funding alignment.

German Ruling Denied Copyright Protection for Logos Produced by GenAI

The local Court in Munich dismissed a copyright claim earlier in February, that purported to assert copyright protection over three logos generated by GenAI. The court, in one of the first European rulings on the matter, held that the logos were not eligible for copyright protection, even though a human allegedly invested many hours in refining the underlying prompts. According to the ruling, copyright protection could theoretically have been granted where despite the use of AI, the design of the logo was the result of a “human creative influence”, and the AI was used as a tool rather than an “independent creative instrument”.

Implication: GenAI output might potentially be copyrightable under very specific circumstances.

NY Federal Judge Asserts AI-Prepared Legal Documents Are Not Subject to Attorney-Client Privilege

Attorney-client privilege protects confidential communications between clients and their lawyers. In February, a federal judge in the Southern District of New York stated during a hearing that documents generated through an AI tool for legal purposes were not privileged.

The court accepted the government's position, whose arguments were based on the fact that the AI tool was not an attorney and that either way its terms of use disclaimed legal advice and privilege.

Implication: Caution advised when using AI for legal drafting.

White House Releases a New National AI Policy Framework

Earlier this March, the White House released a National AI Policy Framework. The Framework outlines both constraints and priorities for AI governance. The Framework calls for safeguards to protect children, American communities, intellectual property, and free speech, while also promoting U.S. leadership in AI, workforce readiness, and the reduction of fragmented state-level regulation. Notably, the Framework urges congressional action in several areas, but with respect to intellectual property it favors resolution through the courts rather than new legislation.

Implication: May shape future federal AI policy while reinforcing a court-led approach to AI and intellectual property disputes.

A Non-Scientist Developed a Personalized Cancer Treatment with the Help of AI

Australian entrepreneur and computer engineer Paul Conyngham, whose dog was dying of cancer, used ChatGPT to help brainstorm a research plan. Having then convinced university researchers to sequence his dog’s DNA, Conyngham used additional AI tools to detect the mutation potentially responsible for his dog’s cancer, and developed an experimental mRNA-based treatment that reportedly improved the dog’s condition significantly.

Implication: AI may lower barriers to exploratory biomedical research, even for people working outside traditional scientific institutions.

Claude Code Deletes Servers, Databases, and Backups During System Update

When a developer was using Claude Code to migrate parts of a cloud setup, Claude generated a plan that resulted in the deletion of the entire production environment, removing critical infrastructure components and 2.5 years of operational data.

Implication: Strong human oversight and technical guardrails remain essential before AI-generated plans are allowed to affect production systems.

Meta Granted a Patent for Simulating Post-Mortem Social Media Activity

Meta was recently granted a patent for a system that could simulate users’ social media activity after their death. The technology would use LLMs trained on a user’s past posts, messages, and interactions to generate new content and respond to others, including mimicking their tone and style.

Implication: AI-based digital afterlife tools could raise new consent, privacy, and ethical questions around a person’s online identity after death.
