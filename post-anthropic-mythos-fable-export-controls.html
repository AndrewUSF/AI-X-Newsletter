Access to Mythos & Fable Models Re-Granted

Category: Policy & Law

Image Source: ChatGPT

On June 12, 2026, the U.S. Department of Commerce issued an export control directive ordering Anthropic to suspend all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 by any foreign national, whether inside or outside the United States, including foreign national Anthropic employees. The reasons associated with the decision were national security threats, with a focus on a fear of “jailbreaking” these models. As a result, and lacking a reliable way to verify nationality in real time, Anthropic disabled both models for all users worldwide to ensure compliance.

The directive followed a report by Amazon researchers who were able to prompt the model to identify software vulnerabilities. According to Anthropic, though, comparable capabilities were widely available from other publicly deployed models.

Anthropic argued that if this standard were applied across the industry, it would essentially halt all new model deployments for all frontier model providers. The resolution came in stages. On June 26, the government approved a partial redeployment of Mythos 5, limited to U.S. organizations working on critical infrastructure defense. On June 30, all export controls were lifted.

As part of the resolution, Anthropic committed to stronger government collaboration including access for evaluation of frontier models, rapid information sharing on significant jailbreaks, and a joint effort along with Amazon, Microsoft, and Google to develop a consensus framework for scoring AI jailbreak severity.

On June 30, White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles posted on X that the White House is working closely with companies on advancing model access and guardrails testing. Anthropic has announced that the Government has lifted the access limitations, and as of July 1 Mythos 5 and Fable 5 have become accessible again.
